## For partners with an overhang objective

Kitagawa is a natural crag in Hanno, Saitama, approached on foot from Nishi-Agano. It suits partners already able to lead and belay outdoors. If your group needs numerous easy lines, examine the distribution before making this the day's venue.

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/北川/) highlights the large overhang and lists chert, or チャート. [ROCKTOPO](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/95) recorded 19 face-climbing lead routes when read on 12 October 2026, including 5.10, 5.11 and 5.12 ranges. This community snapshot is not an access list, and a few easier lines do not guarantee availability for a beginner group.

## Read about descent and cleaning first

The publisher distinguishes the finishing anchors above the overhang from anchors used for retrieval, and records rope drag, old slings and ageing protection. These descriptions help identify questions to ask; they do not replace an actual topo or on-site judgment.

Before travelling, confirm length, finish, descent and equipment recovery for your shortlist. If cleaning an overhanging route is unfamiliar, its grade alone is not a reason to start it. Arrange knowledgeable support or choose an objective you can manage from start to finish.

## Walk from Nishi-Agano with time in hand

The publisher's outline starts at Nishi-Agano on the Seibu railway, passes Zenshoji and reaches the Nakagumi community hall, with a path beside it. Walking estimates differ within the page, so follow the route outline without relying on a guaranteed number of minutes. Rail and walking also avoid the limited local parking.

Pack water and lunch and identify the return train and a descent deadline before setting out. The guide records residents' request that climbers leave before dark. End attempts early enough to pack and walk while there is still daylight.

## Payment and quiet use are part of the arrangement

[JFA's May 2025 notice](https://freeclimb.jp/access/10214/) relays residents' concerns about unpaid agreed parking charges of ¥500 per car and inefficient parking in a small space. It is a dated local request, not a promise of a future rate. Confirm current payment instructions and parking arrangements; if full, change plans rather than use the roadside.

The publisher explains that the crag is also a place of local belief and climbing depends on landowner and community understanding. Avoid shouting, flames, abandoned equipment and excessive chalk marks; greet residents and explain your visit. Paying for parking does not remove those responsibilities.

Use and parking conditions for your actual visit date have not been confirmed. The publisher's spring and autumn preference helps with planning, but recheck notices, rainfall and on-site instructions before your climbing day.
