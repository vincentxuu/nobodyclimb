## Who it suits and what to choose first

Jogasaki is a collection of coastal sectors around Ito in Shizuoka, rather than a single wall with one entrance. [ROCKTOPO's Jogasaki page](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/9) lists crack leads, face leads, boulders and multipitch routes. [Local guide TOMMY CLIMBING CLUB](https://tommy-climbing.com/rocks/cn8/pg257.html) describes the coast from Futo to Akazawa as predominantly crack climbing. Choose a sector and climbing style before choosing your arrival station.

If cracks are your aim, assess your ability to place and evaluate natural protection, select the appropriate rack, establish belays and retreat. An indoor lead grade does not answer those questions. If these skills are new, arrange a guide familiar with the selected sector and make the day an introduction to cracks rather than an independent attempt on a seemingly easy route.

## Choosing sectors and routes

ROCKTOPO identifies the rock as andesite and separates crack from face routes; Jogasaki should not be treated as primarily a sport crag. Names such as Purple Shadow, listed at 5.8, and Peter Pan, listed at 5.10b, are useful leads for locating a topo, rather than approved recommendations for your day. TOMMY's Yawatano information distinguishes natural protection from bolted routes, with mixed protection on some lines. Seeing bolts does not establish that quickdraws alone are sufficient.

## Travel and approach: plan by sector

TOMMY uses Izu-Kogen Station as a meeting point for its Yawatano outings and mentions accommodation around Izu-Kogen, Jogasaki-Kaigan and Futo. That is the guide's arrangement, not a shared entrance for every sector. Before booking, agree on the selected sector's entrance, walking route, any rappel approach and the exit. Exact trains from Tokyo and a complete door-to-door travel time have not been verified here; do not treat a travel note's estimate as a timetable.

## Coastal conditions and a realistic day

TOMMY notes that anchors on Jogasaki cracks are mostly built on trees, and warns that fixed gear left by the sea deteriorates and corrodes badly and should be checked carefully. Check sea conditions, tides, wind and the actual stance, even if photographs show dry rock. If you cannot assess the coastal setting, do not rappel in before establishing an exit. ROCKTOPO highlights December through April as its season reference, which does not guarantee conditions on a particular day. For an April trip, keep the outdoor day flexible rather than tying your only climbing day to a sea cliff.

Suggested example: stay in Izu the previous night and visit one confirmed sector in the morning. Make learning the approach, checking protection and trying one or two conservative objectives the core of the day. If conditions are unsuitable, switch to a coastal walk or indoor climbing. This is an editorial planning example, not a verified guiding package or a route-specific gear list.
