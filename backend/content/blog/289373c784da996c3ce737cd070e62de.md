## The objective is a functioning rope team

Koshizawa Buttress is an Okutama multipitch crag for teams already able to manage belays, bring up a second and descend. Multipitch climbing divides a route into stages with intermediate belays. Its time and technical demands differ from a single sport pitch belayed from the ground.

[CLIMBING-net](https://www.climbing-net.com/iwaba_detail/越沢バットレス/) describes a wall approximately 80 metres high and 70 metres wide, primarily used for multipitch climbing and training for mountain routes. Those dimensions explain the wall's scale; they do not establish a particular route's climbing length or the ropes required. Plan a suitable complete outing instead of a day of accumulating grades.

## An easy grade is only one piece of information

[ROCKTOPO's area page](https://www.rocktopo.net/areas/102) categorizes its recorded main lines as multipitch. A database is not a complete operating manual. Names and grades are insufficient if you cannot identify intermediate belays and descents.

Establish who will lead, who can belay the second, who can assess anchors and how you will communicate out of sight. Keep cancelling the climb as a valid option. If one person is the sole keeper of all the systems, practise in a controlled setting or arrange appropriate instruction first.

## Assess rock edges and fixed protection separately

The publisher questions the reliability of fixed protection. It also warns of sharp edges and past rope-cutting accidents. These issues are distinct from movement difficulty: being able to climb the moves does not establish that you can manage an unsafe rope path or unreliable protection.

Build the equipment list around the intended route and current conditions, including protection, anchors, descent and personal protection. Do not copy a quickdraw count from an unrelated sport crag. This article does not teach anchor construction or descent procedures. Review the topo and retreat options with someone experienced, and do not enter a section you cannot assess.

## Plan the approach and return as part of the climb

Identify the start, approach, descent and exit together. They may follow different paths, so a saved wall coordinate is insufficient. Public-transport visitors need the actual outbound and return services for their date, plus a turnaround time that leaves room for retreat. Sorting equipment, locating the line and waiting for other teams all take time.

A [Miroku mountaineering club report from May 2024](https://npo.mirokuyamanokai.org/2024/06/07/56265) records approximately 30 minutes from Hatonosu Station to the climb. Use Hatonosu as a transport-planning point, but treat the time as that team's observation, not a guarantee of current trail passage or your walking speed. Check trains for your date and obtain the current approach and descent description.

CLIMBING-net mentions a maintenance contribution, but the present fee, approach facilities and fixed-protection condition were not verified in this research; confirm them with relevant management or climbers familiar with the area before departure, rather than treating an older description as a current guarantee. If this information is missing, obtain it before committing the day.

Choose Koshizawa Buttress for a purposeful day using the full skills of mountain climbing. Bring a plan your team can manage independently, leave room for retreat and retain another activity if conditions do not support the climb.
